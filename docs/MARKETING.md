# MARKETING
*Doomscroll4 — brand, voice, and campaign reference*

> This doc captures brand identity, the gremlin character, and campaign strategy. It should inform all copy, landing pages, and social presence. Pricing and distribution tactics live in `DOOMSCROLL4_PLAN.md`.

---

## Tagline

> **Feed your gremlin.**

Primary tagline. Used everywhere — Chrome Web Store listing, landing page hero, social bio.

Secondary line beneath it where space allows:
> *Scroll smarter. Not harder.*

Together they do two things: the first line sells the character and the mechanic. The second line sells the outcome.

---

## The Gremlin — GREM

GREM is the mascot and the product metaphor in one. The user isn't filtering their feed — they're feeding a creature that *eats* bad posts. That reframe is important: it makes filtering feel satisfying rather than restrictive.

**GREM's role in the product:**
- Idle in the toolbar icon, watching
- Comes alive (chomping animation) every time a post gets eaten
- Gets "hungrier" the longer you scroll without bad posts appearing
- Shows a satisfied state after a big eating session

**GREM's personality:**
Chaotic little monster. Mischievous and unpredictable. Not cute in a soft way — feral, with teeth. The kind of creature that would absolutely destroy a sponsored post without hesitation and feel great about it.

**GREM never:**
- Apologizes for eating something
- Explains itself
- Acts polished or corporate

**GREM always:**
- Has an opinion
- Is slightly hungry
- Looks vaguely unhinged in the best way

## Brand Voice

**One sentence:** The product knows you have a bad habit and isn't here to lecture you about it.

**Tone references:**
- Duolingo's unhinged energy, but darker
- Early Notion — self-aware, a little dry
- The kind of thing an engineer would build for themselves and then reluctantly ship

**In practice:**

| Instead of... | Say... |
|---|---|
| "Improve your social media experience" | "Stop eating garbage. Feed Grem instead." |
| "Filter unwanted content" | "Grem handles the slop." |
| "Your personalized feed settings" | "Tell Grem what to eat." |
| "Content you may find relevant" | "Grem thinks you'll like this." |
| "Hidden post" | "Eaten." |

**Never use:**
- "AI-powered" as a standalone descriptor (meaningless now)
- "Curate" — too LinkedIn
- "Mindful" — too wellness
- "Productivity" — too corporate
- Exclamation points in serious UI — reserve for GREM moments only

## Campaign: Doomscroll4X

Each sub-campaign targets a different audience segment with tailored copy. Same product, same gremlin, different angle.

### Doomscroll4learning
**Audience:** Students, lifelong learners, curious people, science Twitter  
**Hook:** You're on here to learn. So why did you just spend 20 minutes watching arguments about nothing?  
**Copy angle:** Feed Grem the noise. Keep the signal.

### Doomscroll4work
**Audience:** Engineers, PMs, and professionals on LinkedIn  
**Hook:** LinkedIn would be useful if it weren't 90% hustle content and humble brags.  
**Copy angle:** Grem eats the engagement bait. You keep the industry intel.

### Doomscroll4science
**Audience:** Researchers, science communicators, STEM community on Twitter/X  
**Hook:** The papers are in there somewhere. Grem will find them.  
**Copy angle:** Cut to the research. Let Grem handle the rest.

### Doomscroll4fun
**Audience:** People who just want the good stuff without the rage  
**Hook:** You didn't come here to be angry. Neither did Grem.  
**Copy angle:** Keep the memes. Lose the outrage. Feed Grem.

## Retention Hook — The Hunger Loop

GREM gets hungrier between sessions. When a user opens their feed after not using the extension for a day:

> *"GREM hasn't eaten in 18 hours. He's getting restless."*

This is a re-engagement mechanic disguised as a character moment. It works because it reframes "you haven't used the extension" as "your gremlin misses you" — which is more compelling and more on-brand than any notification copy.

## What Doomscroll4 Is Not

Useful to have this written down to keep copy from drifting:

- **Not a screen time app.** We don't track how long you scroll. We don't want you to scroll less necessarily — we want you to scroll better.
- **Not a news aggregator.** We don't pull content. We filter what's already there.
- **Not a parental control.** The user controls GREM. GREM works for them.
- **Not preachy.** We never imply the user has a problem. GREM has the problem — GREM is hungry, GREM needs feeding. The user is the one in control.
